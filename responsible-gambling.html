Responsible gambling

Betting should be fun. It should never be a way to pay bills, win back losses, or escape a bad day.

Need help right now?

1-800-GAMBLER

Free, private, 24 hours a day, every day. You can also text 800GAM or chat online at ncpgambling.org (National Council on Problem Gambling).

Signs it may be a problem

  • Betting more money, or more often, than you planned.
  • Chasing losses: betting bigger to win back what you lost.
  • Borrowing money, or skipping bills, to bet.
  • Lying about how much you bet, or feeling guilty or anxious about it.
  • Thinking about betting most of the day, or getting irritable when you cut back.
  • Betting is hurting your work, sleep, health or relationships.

Habits that keep it fun

  • Set a budget before you bet and treat it as money spent on entertainment. When it is gone, stop.
  • Never chase losses. A bad day is not a reason to bet bigger.
  • Keep stakes small. VetoBet's bankroll tool suggests sizes that keep a bad run from hurting.
  • Take breaks. Set a time limit, and do other things you enjoy.
  • Do not bet when upset, tired or after drinking.
  • Remember: even a good price does not guarantee a win. Over many bets better prices help, but any single bet can lose.

Tools you can use today

  • At your sportsbook: every licensed US sportsbook lets you set deposit limits, loss limits and time-outs, and take a break or close your account. Look under "Responsible gaming" in their app.
  • Self-exclusion: many states run official self-exclusion lists that bar you from every licensed sportsbook in that state. Search for "[your state] sports betting self-exclusion".
  • Block gambling sites: free tools such as Gamban and BetBlocker can block gambling sites and apps on your devices.
  • On VetoBet: you can delete your account at any time from the account box, and we do not take bets or hold money, so there is nothing to withdraw.

If someone you know is struggling

Talk to them without blame, offer to listen, and share the helpline above. Support groups such as Gamblers Anonymous (gamblersanonymous.org) are free. The helpline also supports family and friends.
